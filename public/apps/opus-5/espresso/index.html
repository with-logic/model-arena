The $4,000 plateau

You bought the grinder. The shots are still inconsistent.

You're not lacking discipline or taste. You're lacking a feedback loop. Every serious craft — wine, woodworking, cycling — has a coach and calibrated equipment. Coffee somehow expects you to learn it alone from YouTube.

01

The gear tax never ends

A capable roaster is $3,500. A pump-driven machine with real temperature stability is $3,000+. Then the grinder, the refractometer, the scale. You've spent a car payment to still not know why Tuesday's shot beat Thursday's.

02

No one is tasting with you

You can't calibrate a palate in isolation. "Slightly sour" means nothing without someone across the table who can name it, tie it to your drop temp, and tell you which variable to move next.

03

You change three things at once

Grind, dose, and temperature — all at the same time, then wonder what worked. Without a protocol you're not experimenting. You're just burning through good coffee.

From signup to a shot you're proud of

Six months, four phases

This is why we ask for six months and not one. Palate calibration takes about eight weeks. Roast intuition takes longer. A 30-day trial teaches you how to unpack a box.

01
Week 1

Palate baseline

We come to you, install the bench, and run a triangulation test to find what your palate actually detects — and what it misses. That result sets your curriculum.

02
Weeks 2–8

One variable at a time

Structured protocols: grind, then dose, then temperature, then pressure. You change one thing, taste the difference blind, and write it down. This is where most people finally break through.

03
Weeks 9–18

Roast development

You take the roaster. Charge temp, gas, airflow, drop. Your coach reads your curves between sessions and sends the next experiment before you ask for it.

04
Weeks 19–26

Your signature espresso

Blend or single origin, built by you, cupped blind against a commercial control. Members who hit 84+ on the SCA form get their lot roasted at production scale — 5 lb, bagged with your name on it.
