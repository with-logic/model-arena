Coriolis introduces Meridian, a solar smartwatch built to outlast the polar night

Sapphire solar glass, up to 21 days per charge and a 64-day Polar mode. Available to pre-order today from $549, shipping from 4 December.

Tromsø, Norway, 13 November 2026. Coriolis today introduced Meridian, a smartwatch that charges from the light that reaches your wrist. A transparent photovoltaic film sits under its sapphire crystal and an opaque solar ring circles the display, so the watch tops up on a walk to work, an afternoon on the water or a long day on a ridge. Meridian comes in two sizes and three titanium finishes.

Meridian runs for up to 21 days on a full battery in smartwatch mode. With three hours of outdoor light a day, it doesn’t need its charger at all. Polar mode keeps time, steps, heart rate and sunrise alerts running for up to 64 days, longer than the polar night in Tromsø, where the sun stays below the horizon from late November to mid January.

The display is built for daylight too. Meridian uses a reflective colour display that gets easier to read as the sun gets brighter, with a warm front light for evenings. Its default face wraps a 24-hour ring around the time, showing where the sun is now and how much daylight is left wherever you are.

The case is machined from grade 5 titanium and is 11.4 mm thin in the 45 mm size. Inside are dual-band GPS with offline topographic maps, optical heart-rate and blood-oxygen sensors, a skin-temperature sensor and a barometric altimeter. Meridian is water resistant to 10 ATM, works from −30 °C to 60 °C, and pairs with iPhone and Android.

Pricing and availability

Meridian 41 mm costs $549 and Meridian 45 mm costs $599; the Basalt finish adds $50. Each watch comes with an Alpine weave or Fjord sport band, and the Titanium link band is $150 more. Pre-orders open today at coriolis.watch with no payment until the watch ships. Deliveries begin 4 December 2026 in Norway, the EU, the UK, the US, Canada and Japan.

About Coriolis

Coriolis makes instruments for people who spend their days outside. The company was founded in Tromsø in 2019 by Ingrid Solberg and Anders Vik, and designs and tests every product inside the Arctic Circle. Meridian is its second watch, after the Coriolis Field in 2023.
