Tree cover is growing fastest where it was thinnest

We choose blocks by heat and need, not by who asks first. Since 2014, tree cover in the eight neighborhoods where we plant most has grown by 4 to 7 percentage points.

Every one of them is still below the citywide average. That is why our 2027 plan puts the most trees in Ironworks and Harbor East.

Nine in ten of our trees now live through their first three summers

Young street trees die most often of thirst. In 2021 we added paid summer water crews to back up our Block Stewards, and survival has climbed with every planting class since.

The bus stop that used to have no shade

In 2019 the Route 14 stop at Delmar and 9th had a bench, a sign and nothing overhead. Neighbors asked us for trees, and the block's stewards watered all 31 of them through three summers. Last July, the sidewalk that once read 131°F at 3 pm read 104°F.

“I used to wait inside the laundromat until I saw the bus turn the corner. Now I sit on the bench and read.”

How we measured

We publish our methods so anyone can check the numbers. Questions about any figure go to our evaluation lead, who answers every one.

  1. Trees planted. Every tree is logged in our inventory at planting, with its species, location and tag number. Ring years in the cross-section are fiscal years; fiscal year 2026 ran July 1, 2025 to June 30, 2026.
  2. Survival. Each summer our crews inspect every tree in its third growing season. A tree counts as alive if at least three-quarters of its crown is healthy. The class of 2023 was 2,610 trees.
  3. Temperature. One sensor per block, 10 feet up on a light pole, on 40 planted blocks and 40 comparison blocks matched for street width, building height and paving. Air figures average every July 2026 day. Sidewalk readings were taken with handheld infrared thermometers on the same days.
  4. Canopy cover. Share of land under tree canopy, from the city's 2014 and 2026 aerial canopy assessments. Gains include trees planted by others and the growth of existing trees.
  5. Tree Corps outcomes. A six-month follow-up survey of all 64 graduates, 61 of whom responded. Working means 20 or more paid hours a week; school includes job training programs.
  6. Finances. From our audited financial statements for fiscal year 2026. Program costs include tree stock, planting, watering, pruning and Tree Corps wages.
