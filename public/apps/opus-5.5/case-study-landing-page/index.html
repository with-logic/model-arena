Eleven days spent proving last month’s numbers

XYZ Corp grew by acquisition. Between 2019 and 2024 it bought five regional HVAC manufacturers, and each arrived with its own ERP, its own chart of accounts and its own way of closing the month. By 2025 a corporate finance team of 38 was consolidating 14 legal entities across the US, Canada, Mexico, Ireland, Poland and Vietnam.

The close ran on a shared drive. Balance sheet reconciliations lived in 14 workbooks with 14 different ideas of what “reviewed” meant. Intercompany balances were agreed by email, which meant waiting on colleagues six time zones away, then reworking eliminations when someone found a mismatch on day eight.

One close, connected to both ERPs

XYZ Corp chose Crossfoot in May 2025 after a six-week evaluation against two other close platforms. Native connectors for NetSuite and SAP Business One decided it: no flat-file exports to maintain, and every balance in Crossfoot ties back to the ledger it came from.

Four days, nearly every month since

The first live close took nine days while the team learned to trust the matching rules. By November 2025 it had reached four, and it has finished on day four every month since, apart from the year-end and March quarter-end closes, which took five.

The time didn’t turn into slack. XYZ Corp moved two staff accountants into FP&A, and the controller’s team now sends variance commentary to business-unit leaders on day five. That report used to arrive around day fifteen, when it arrived at all.
